# Accessibility Notes & Known Issues

This document lists accessibility and mobile-usability constraints that were identified during the WCAG 2.2 AA audit but were deemed unsafe or impractical to fix in the initial pass.

## Canvas Gameplay Accessibility

- **Non-Text Content / Canvas:** The primary gameplay is rendered on a `<canvas>` element. While the canvas itself has an `aria-label="Game"`, the internal objects (enemies, platforms, items) are completely procedural and lack DOM representations. As a result, screen readers cannot access or describe the gameplay state in real-time.
- **Audio Cues:** Gameplay currently relies heavily on visual cues. The audio cues are not comprehensive enough to make the game playable by someone with severe visual impairments (e.g., no spatial audio to identify enemy positions or incoming projectiles).

## DJ Booth and Spotify Integration

- **Spotify Embed:** The Spotify component embedded in the DJ Booth relies on a third-party iframe, over which the game has limited control. Certain interactions inside the embed may have their own accessibility quirks outside of this project's scope.
- **Beat Matching Visuals:** Beat matching relies on visual waveforms and tempo reading, which are currently inaccessible to screen-reader users without DOM descriptions.

## Screen Contrast

- **Decorative Glows:** Various in-game particle effects and neon elements use bright, glowy colors against the dark background. Some fast-moving particles may present contrast edges that temporarily dip below WCAG guidelines, but they are decorative rather than informative.

## Next Steps

For a future major release, we could explore exposing an accessible DOM representation or 'live region' text feed for events happening inside the `<canvas>` context, or adding a high-contrast mode for gameplay graphics.
